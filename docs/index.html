Living PRD (Product Requirements Document)

Updated every week as we test with real users and learn what works.

1. Target User & The Job to Be Done

Who is this for? NYU Stern MBA classmates who read at least one book a month for pleasure, starting with me. I can reach them through class, clubs and group chats.

The painful "Before" workflow: After finishing a book they loved, they look for the next one on Instagram, Goodreads, Amazon or bookstore tables. Every channel pushes the same bestsellers, and star ratings say nothing about why a book worked for them. They give up and pick whatever is trending, or nothing.

The "After" workflow with this product: In about five minutes they fill in a short reflection template (three words for the book, the one moment they still remember, what it changed in them). Hidden Shelf turns that into a taste profile and returns five lesser-known books from a large open catalog, each with a reason.

2. Scope & Explicit Non-Goals

  • In Scope (Core Workflow): Pick a book you loved → write the structured reflection → get five hidden-gem recommendations with a "why you" explanation.
  • Out of Scope #1: Points or rewards for reviews that can be used to borrow or buy books.
  • Out of Scope #2: Social features: book clubs, following other readers, comments.
  • Out of Scope #3: Our own book database and affiliate shopping links. We use the free Open Library catalog instead.

3. How It Works Under the Hood & Riskiest Assumption (Week 2+)

Riskiest Assumption: That a structured reflection actually leads to better recommendations than star ratings, and that readers will take five minutes to write one.

Simple Architecture: [Book + reflection] → [Browser app] → [Open Library search API; AI model from Build 2] → [Five hidden gems + reasons]
